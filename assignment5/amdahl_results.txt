1. How do your measured speedups compare to the ideal linear speedup?

The measured speedups were slighly higher than the ideal linear speedups until reaching 8 threads. After 8 threads, the 
measured speedups fall below the ideal linear speedup. This is due to the effects of synchronization and communication between 
threads or overhead.


2. What value of P do you estimate from your data? What parts of the program contribute to the serial fraction 1- P?

I would say about 93% - 98%. The parts of the program that contributes to the serial fraction 1-P is the creating, launching, and joining the std::thread.
Another part is allocating threads and partial results. The final part is combining the partial sums at the end with a for loop.


3. Why does efficiency decrease as the number of threads grows?

Efficiency decreases as the number of threads grow because the serial portion of the code limits total speedup, parallel overheads grows faster due to 
synchronization and communication, and threads start competing for shared resources.


4. Identify at least two practical sources of overhead that Amdahl’s Law does not explicitly model.

Thread joining and load imbalance.


5. Suppose you ran this program on a machine with 32 or 64 cores. Predict how the speedup curve would behave and explain why.

The speedup curve with 32 would flatten asymptotically. This is because the serial portion of the equation would dominate. If the number of cores 
increase to 64, the result would be the same, with a slighly increased, but not significant speed up time.
